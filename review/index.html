1

The short version

What's working

The content is strong. There are real stories from right across the Communion, a photo archive most charities would love to have, resources in five languages, staff based in eight countries, and a clear way of explaining the work in the vision wheel. The site was rebuilt on WordPress at the start of 2025, which is a sound base, and the search engine basics are in place.

What's getting in the way

On a phone the homepage downloads 31 MB, and in Google's test on a slow 4G connection the main image takes about 14 seconds to appear. There's no way to give or get in touch from the phone menu, the resources are long lists of links, 18 links on the main pages go nowhere, and some content has drifted out of date.

What I'd do

Fix the foundations first, inside the current site, which takes a few weeks. Then redesign the key pages around what people actually come to do, with the wheel as the backbone, and add a directory of the agencies and a page for each major emergency, so the network the Alliance holds together can be seen. Languages and a simpler way to give follow on from there.

3

What's worth keeping

The vision wheel

It already gives each priority area its own colour and icon, and shows how the five approaches run through all of them. At the moment it sits on three inner pages as an image. I'd make it the backbone of the whole site, and the concept shows how.

The photography

The 42 “Hope and light” photographs show the work happening in real places, with captions that name them. They deserve better than an autoplaying carousel.

The people

Staff in eight countries, and regional facilitators that people can contact directly. That's a real strength for a small team, and it isn't obvious on the site today.

Resources in five languages

People on the Move is available in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French and Arabic. That's unusual for a team your size, and worth making more of.

A sound platform

WordPress on good hosting (Kinsta behind Cloudflare), search basics in place and best-practice scores of 100. Nothing here needs throwing away.

4

What I found

I've grouped these by the part of someone's visit they affect, and marked how soon I'd deal with each one. “Fix first” means it's costing you visitors or gifts now, “fix soon” means it's holding the site back, and “tidy up” covers smaller things that add up.

Fix firstFix soonTidy up

Speed

Fix first

The homepage is 31 MB

Most of it is the “Hope and light” carousel: 42 photographs at full size, 23 MB between them, all downloaded as soon as the page opens even though most people only see the first three. The three “In focus” cards also load 2,560-pixel images into boxes about 300 pixels wide. For partners on prepaid mobile data, in Fiji or South Sudan say, that's a real cost.

Fix: show the photographs as a gallery that loads as people scroll, at the size they're displayed.

Fix soon

Every page carries the page builder's weight

The homepage loads 44 scripts and 26 stylesheets, and inner pages around 30 and 20. That includes the Raleway font in 18 weights and Open Sans in 10, on top of the Europa font the site actually uses. Pages that aren't already cached can take more than a second just to start arriving.

Fix: a lighter theme removes most of this (see the plan). In the meantime the unused fonts can be switched off.

Phones

Fix first

No way to give or get in touch from a phone

On a phone, “Support our work” and “Contact” disappear from the header, and the menu lists Privacy, Cookies, Accessibility and Disclaimer instead. The footer doesn't have them either.

Fix: keep a Give button in the phone header and put Contact in the menu, as the concept does (compare the phones at the top of this review).

Fix first

Zoom is switched off

The site tells phones not to let people pinch to zoom (maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0). For anyone whose eyesight isn't perfect that's a real barrier, and it fails the accessibility guidelines.

Fix: a one-line change.

Fix soon

The first screen doesn't say who you are

On a phone, the first screen is the menu bar, the logo with a lot of white space around it, a yellow strip and the top of the slider. The sentence explaining what the Alliance does starts on the second screen, and until someone answers it, the cookie banner covers most of the first.

Fix: lead with one photograph, one sentence about the Alliance and two clear next steps.

Finding things

Fix soon

The menu follows the organisation chart

Three of the six menu items describe the organisation (Who we are, What we do, How we do it), with 20 pages between them. The things people come for, like a toolkit, this month's prayer focus, a regional contact or a way to give, are spread across them.

Fix: a menu built around those tasks, with the wheel bringing What and How onto one page. More in section 6.

Fix soon

Resources are hard to browse

The Resources page is five accordions of plain links, and the Toolkits page repeats many of them. Some resources sit under three subjects. There's no way to filter by language or type, and no file sizes, so someone on a phone can't tell that the Myanmar consultation report is a 23.8 MB download, or that four others are over 10 MB.

Fix: one library with filters for work area, type and language, showing the format and size of every item.

Fix soon

Some things look clickable but aren't

The 42 “Hope and light” photographs are set up as clickable cards that go nowhere. The homepage slides and “In focus” cards only work through JavaScript, so they can't be reached with a keyboard and search engines can't follow them, and the “In focus” cards open your own pages in a new tab.

Fix: make every card a real link to a real page.

Tidy up

The site has more pages than it needs

The sitemap lists 208 pages. Some are duplicates with identical titles, such as /hazy-days-are-here-again/ and /hazy-days-are-here-again-2/. Older sections are still live with 20 or 30 words on them (/relief/, /development/, /advocacy-2/), /map/ is empty, login pages are listed, and /community/ shows a paragraph of Lorem ipsum placeholder text to anyone who finds it. Its directory, login and registration pages show raw plugin code, so it looks as if a members' directory was started and never finished.

Fix: redirect what has moved, remove what's empty, and keep the sitemap to pages you'd want people to land on.

Tidy up

Two agency lists that disagree

The Anglican agencies page lists 17 agencies and the Partnership page lists 13, with different agencies on each and an old name on one (PWRDF, which is now Alongside Hope). On the agencies page, 19 of the 25 images, mostly logos, have no description for screen readers.

Fix: one directory, kept in one place. It's phase 3 of the plan, and the concept has a working version.

Flagship programmes

Fix soon

Agents of Change deserves a better page

Agents of Change is one of the Alliance's touchstone resources, and its page has drifted. The "Application Form" link opens the introductory guide for distance learners, while the page says the course isn't taking distance learners, and two different introductory guides are linked. The contact address changes between the text and the link (@anglicanalliance.org and @anglicancommunion.org). The page still describes "year 1 of the grant" of a three-year roll-out, the overview film is a 35 MB download, and the four videos sit behind the cookie notice. The course's own diagram, with the stages of a project round the outside and the principles of good practice in the middle, only appears inside the PDF.

Fix: give each flagship programme a page built to one pattern: what it is, how it works, what people say and how to take part, all kept current. The concept has a working version for Agents of Change.

Giving and getting in touch

Fix first

Giving relies on a QR code

The donate page asks people to scan a QR code or copy a long Stewardship link. That's awkward on a laptop and impossible on the phone you're reading it on. One news story sends people to a separate JustGiving page instead, and on the donate page itself the “Support our work” button loses its text.

Fix: a proper giving page where people choose a fund, see what it does and go through to Stewardship with one button.

Fix soon

Contact details disagree

Paulo Ueti appears at three addresses on the Contact and Staff pages, one with a typo (anglicanalliance.orrg). Clifton Nedd and Karen Chute have different addresses on the two pages, the general enquiries address on Contact differs from the one on the mailing list page, and Gay Manadon's surname is spelt two ways.

Fix: one list of people, kept in one place and shown wherever it's needed.

Accessibility

Fix soon

Contrast and page structure

The main “Support our work” button is white on light green at a contrast of 2.66 to 1, well under the 4.5 to 1 minimum. The homepage has no main heading and 53 second-level ones, and none of the 19 pages I checked has a skip link or a main landmark for screen readers.

Fix: built into the new templates, and most of it can be fixed in the current theme too.

Tidy up

Text inside pictures

The staff team map has every name, role and location built into the image, so it can't be read aloud, searched or updated without a designer. The same goes for the Bible verse on the Prayer Room photograph.

Fix: use real text, as the concept does with its map of where the team is based.

Tidy up

Videos hidden behind the cookie settings

Both videos in the Prayer Room show “Click to accept marketing cookies and enable this content” until a visitor accepts marketing cookies.

Fix: a privacy-friendly video player, or a still image that links to the video.

Keeping it current

Fix first

18 links go nowhere

I checked the 233 links on the 35 main pages and 18 are broken. They include all three Safe Church guides, “God's Justice: Theology and Gender Based Violence” (listed twice), The Freedom Framework and “Prayers for the Peoples of the World”. Several are WordPress preview links, which only work for someone logged in. The Anglican Missions link on the Anglican agencies page points at an old address that now shows a security warning.

Fix: repair these now, then run a link check every month.

Tidy up

Some pages have drifted

The footer still says 2025. Who we are and What we do both quote people who have since moved on. The Communion Forest page calls it “an exciting new initiative” four years after its launch. The Prayer Room says this month's focus is tackling inequalities while the prayers underneath are about disasters, and it doesn't say which month it means.

Fix: a short checklist each quarter, and a date on anything time-bound.

The concept

I've built a working homepage concept from your own words and photographs. Nearly everything on it is real content from the current site, lightly edited. It also has a page for Agents of Change, built around the course's own diagram, and two pages the site doesn't have today, an agency directory and an example emergency page, which are phase 3 of the plan. It's a starting point for a conversation rather than a finished design.

The colours come from your wheel. Each priority area keeps its own colour, which shows up as a small wedge on every story, resource and photograph, and the wheel's pointer is the compass star from your logo. The headings use Jost, a free typeface close to the Europa you already use, and the real site would keep Europa.

Try it on your phone as well as a laptop. The wheel, resource filters, search and menu all work.

The plan

I'd do this in four phases, so you see a difference quickly and the cost can be spread out. My recommendation is to keep WordPress. Your team knows it, the content and the translation plugin are already there, and the hosting is good. What I'd replace is the Divi page builder, which is where most of the weight comes from and what makes the pages fiddly to keep consistent.

Phase 3 is the one thing the site doesn't do at all yet. You describe the Alliance as sitting “at the centre of a web of relationships”, and when a disaster strikes you help the local church shape “a proposal/appeal that partner churches and agencies can support”. None of that shows on the site today. A directory of the agencies and a page for each major emergency would show it, and send people who want to give to the agencies that can take their gift. The concept has a working version of both: the agency directory and an example page for the 2025 Myanmar earthquake, built from your own report.

1. Fix the foundations

About 3 weeks, inside the current site

  • A lighter homepage gallery, and images at the size they're shown
  • Give and Contact on phones, and zoom switched back on
  • Button contrast, headings, a skip link and real links on cards
  • The 18 broken links, then a monthly link check
  • Redirects for duplicate and old pages, and a tidy sitemap
  • One set of contact details
  • The out-of-date items in section 4

Aim: the homepage down from 31 MB to under 5 MB, and nothing broken.

2. Redesign

About 8 to 10 weeks

  • A short discovery with your team, and a couple of partners, to check the audiences and tasks
  • The new structure, and a design system built from the wheel
  • A lightweight custom WordPress theme in place of Divi, with templates for the homepage, work areas, programmes, stories, the resource library, people and regions, the Prayer Room and giving, built so it could be shared later (see section 9)
  • The resource library set up with type, work area, language and file size for each item (about 60 resources)
  • Programme pages for Agents of Change, Partners in Resilience and Response, the Resilience Course and Reimagining Our World Together, all built to one pattern
  • Accessibility and speed checks before launch, and training for whoever edits the site

Aim: pages under 1 MB, accessibility scores of 95 or more, and the same content and editors you have now.

3. Agency directory and response pages

About 2 weeks, once the new templates are in place

  • An agency directory: a profile for every agency, with its work in the wheel's colours, where it's based and its latest news where it publishes a feed
  • A short form so agencies keep their own profile up to date, with the Alliance checking each change
  • A response page for each major emergency: what's happened, what the local church is doing, prayer points, how the Alliance is helping, and the agencies' appeals, marked closed when they end
  • A private space for agencies later, but only if the public pages get used

Aim: one place to see the Anglican response to a disaster, and to give through an agency in your own country.

4. Grow

Ongoing, a step at a time

  • Spanish, Portuguese, French and Arabic for the key pages and resource records, using the translation plugin you already have
  • A stories archive by region and work area, starting with the Hope and light photographs
  • Regular giving and clearer fund choices, working with Stewardship
  • A short quarterly report on what people use and where they come from

Aim: a site that partners can use in their own language.

9

Where this could go

One idea to put on the table rather than in the plan. What if the agencies' own sites could share the same foundations? Each agency would keep its own name, look and voice, and nothing about how it raises money or looks after its supporters would change. Underneath, the sites would use the same tags for the five work areas and the regions, the same kinds of content (stories, appeals, resources, emergencies) and some of the same building blocks.

That would let stories and appeals move between sites without anyone retyping them, give each major disaster one page that every agency's site could show, and let an agency that needs a new site start from parts that already exist. I'd build the Alliance's new theme so this stays possible, and phase 3 would be the first step. I've kept it out of the plan and costs, as it's something to talk through with the agencies rather than a decision for the Alliance alone.
